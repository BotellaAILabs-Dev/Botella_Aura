## 2. Guided onboarding — `94e3926`

Setup was a 3-step wizard ending in **"You're all set"** — which was untrue, Aura knew the company name and nothing else — followed by four suggestion links with no order and no completion tracking.

Now seven ordered steps, each linking to a real working surface:

1. Add your business information → the wizard
2. Upload your marketing materials → knowledge base
3. Add your brand assets → **new page**
4. Define your audience and goals → **new page**
5. Connect your marketing channels → connections
6. Generate your first marketing plan → planner
7. Review your first week of work → the briefing

**Completion is derived, never stored.** There is no `onboardingStep` column to drift out of sync — a step is done when the thing it asks for is in the database. Delete your last knowledge source and that step correctly reopens.

**Exactly one step is ever the next action** — the first incomplete one, and the only one with a button.

Steps 3 and 4 pointed nowhere, so they were built. The logo upload goes through the *exact* pipeline a post graphic does — magic-number MIME sniff, size and dimension ceilings, S3-or-database storage, and the asset route that already refuses to serve outside its company. The only difference is `contentDraftId: null`.

Neither new field is write-only:

- Brand colours reach the **image prompt** as palette direction
- The audience sentence reaches **every draft**, verbatim and ahead of anything inferred
- Goals steer what gets written, with an explicit rule never to state them in the post
- Audience, goals and channels **pre-fill the first marketing plan**

Both live in `Company.preferences` — **no migration**.

## 3. Business materials — `6045fcb`

Two things blocked this.

**The formats did not parse.** A pitch deck is a `.pptx`; brand guidelines are usually a `.docx`. The upload route rejected both outright — the single most valuable document a company owns could not be given to Aura at all.

**Nothing knew what a document *was*.** The schema recorded where a chunk came from, never what it is. The page stating what makes you different competed on keyword overlap with a stale blog post — and a 30-day recency half-life was quietly decaying the brand guidelines Aura is supposed to obey.

`.docx` and `.pptx` now parse. Both are ZIP archives of XML, read directly rather than via a dependency — Node's `zlib` already does inflate. Slides sort **numerically** (slide 2 before slide 10, which string sorting reverses for any deck over nine slides), paragraph boundaries survive, and **speaker notes are kept** and filed under their slide — in a real deck the slide has three words and the notes carry the argument.

Nine material kinds, each changing behaviour rather than acting as a badge:

| Kind | Weight | Ages out? |
|---|---|---|
| Key differentiators | 1.6 | never |
| Target audience / ICP | 1.5 | never |
| Brand guidelines | 1.4 | never |
| Pitch decks | 1.35 | never |
| Existing content & campaigns | 1.25 | yes |
| Company/product information | 1.2 | never |
| Competitor information | 1.15 | yes |
| Product documentation | 1.1 | yes |

*(Logo/visual assets ship as brand assets from commit 2.)*

- **Reference material no longer decays.**
- **What a document is outranks where it came from.**
- **Excerpts are labelled and grouped in the prompt.** Guidelines arrive as `BRAND RULES (follow these exactly)`; competitor material arrives explicitly marked never to be adopted as ours — that one was failing silently in the worst way available: fluent copy repeating a rival's marketing as your own claim.

Uploading asks what the document is *after* the file is picked, so the question can name it and pre-select from the filename. The guess is never applied silently.

The page now reports which kinds Aura has and which it lacks, counting **chunks not sources** — a site that ingested nothing readable is not coverage.

## 4. SIPN, and the B2C/B2B fork — `b88ea1c`

Setting SIPN up exposed a hard-coded assumption: **every writing rule was written for a business buyer, and applied to everything.**

> *"Every claim should connect to something they are measured on — revenue, cost, risk, time, or team capacity. If a sentence would not change how they spend their week, cut it."*

Right for procurement, **actively destructive for bourbon**. A drinker is not measured on anything and is not accountable to a boss for the purchase. Worse, an unnamed reader was described to the model as *"a busy business decision-maker"* — the instruction it leans on hardest when nothing else is specified.

Rules now fork on who is buying. Shared rules stay shared; the rest splits:

**B2B** — lead with the business consequence · explain the so-what · technical detail only where it explains an outcome · no *leverage/robust/seamless*

**B2C** — lead with the experience, not the spec sheet · craft detail welcome but tie it to what it *does* · write to one person, never *about* an audience · no corporate voice · no category clichés (*smooth, artisanal, premium, "a journey"*) · end with **one human ask** · **never invent an age statement, award or provenance** — a regulated category makes that a legal problem, not a stylistic one

An unset mode resolves to B2B, so nothing changes for existing workspaces. The selector reads **"Who buys from you?"** — the B2B/B2C jargon is ours, not the user's — and the audience/goals examples follow the choice.

**SIPN ships as two workspaces, not one with a toggle.** Bourbon sells bottles to drinkers; Enterprises sells shelf slots to distributors. Each needs its own knowledge base, brand voice, audience, goals, palette and results.

```
pnpm seed:sipn      sipn@botella.ai / Password123
```

Real configuration and real materials — brand guidelines, differentiators, ICP and app docs on the consumer side; partner deck, differentiators, ICP and competitor notes on the trade side. **No fabricated drafts or invented metrics.**

> **Bug found:** `getAudienceProfile` returned `null` when a workspace had set only a market mode — throwing away the most important thing it had been told, and falling back to writing for a business buyer. The exact mistake the field exists to prevent.

## 5. Publishing — `f537101`

Audited the seven capabilities before building. Four already existed; one is partial; **two were genuinely missing**, and they were the same gap from both ends.

| Capability | State |
|---|---|
| Understand the business | already there |
| Generate content and creatives | already there |
| **Recommend when/where to publish** | **built today** |
| **Approval or auto-publish by settings** | **built today** |
| Monitor performance | already there |
| Identify marketing gaps | partial — see below |
| Proactively recommend and prepare | already there |

Aura wrote a week of posts and left every one with **no date**, handing the scheduling decision back to the user for work they had asked it to take off their hands.

**When.** `recommendSlot()` uses the workspace's own published posts and returns the reason with the slot:

- A day that is merely first is not a finding — it must beat the average by a real margin
- One spectacular Saturday cannot become *"post on Saturdays"*
- The hour is a **median**, so a single 3am post cannot drag the slot into the night
- With no data it falls back to a platform convention **and says so**
- A batch spreads across days rather than stacking

**Whether.** Three settings, named for what happens:

- **review** — write drafts and wait *(default, and the existing behaviour)*
- **schedule** — also put them on the calendar; a human still approves
- **auto** — approve and publish without asking

Every guard on `auto` exists because the failure is public and irreversible: never anyone's default · an unrecognised stored value falls back to *review* · requires a connected channel · capped per week regardless of config · needs the same permission a human needs to approve a post, because that is the authority being delegated · setting changes and every self-approval hit the audit log. The UI says plainly that posts go out without anyone reading them, and asks for a confirmation that **re-arms every time** the mode is selected afresh.

It reuses the existing publisher — auto mode approves, and the scheduled-publish job that has always handled APPROVED drafts does the rest. **No new code can publish.**

Autopilot also had **no settings UI at all** — JSON in a column, unreachable without a database client. It has one now.
